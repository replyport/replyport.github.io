What ReplyPort is

A small Windows program that sits between your AI assistant and the Outlook mailbox you already use.

It runs on your PC, next to Outlook. When you ask your assistant for a reply, ReplyPort is the part that touches your mailbox: it creates the draft, checks it is on the right thread and addressed to the right person, and then stops. There is no automation waiting to fire, no queue of scheduled messages, and no send button anywhere in ReplyPort.

Your mail stays yours
We keep no copy of your mailbox on our servers.
You choose the folders
Grant access per folder, and change it whenever you like.
Outlook doesn't change
No add-in ribbon, no new inbox to learn.

Three steps, and one of them is yours.

The split is deliberate. The AI understands and prepares. ReplyPort does the mailbox work locally. Sending stays a human act.

The boundary, written down

Neither the AI nor ReplyPort can send email.

Not as a setting you can switch on. The ability simply isn't built.

What the AI, ReplyPort and you can each do
Capability AI ReplyPort You
Read the threads you allow Yes Yes Yes
Compose draft text Yes No Yes
Write and verify the draft in Outlook No Yes, locally Yes
Send No No Only you

“Yes, locally” means the work happens in the ReplyPort process on your machine, against the Outlook profile already signed in there.

Every draft ReplyPort writes is checked against the thread it belongs to and the recipients on it before it appears in Drafts.

The mark tells you where it is

One square, one stroke, three honest states.

The logo doubles as the tray icon. The stroke rests at 45°, turns while ReplyPort is preparing something, and settles upright when a draft is waiting for you. If you've asked your system to reduce motion, the stroke never turns — the state is shown by the frame instead.

Idle

Connected, nothing to do.

Working

Preparing a draft. Smooth rotation, one full turn per 2.6s.

Draft ready

Motion stops upright. Your turn.

Runs on the Windows PC where your Outlook already lives.
